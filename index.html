2 · My ultimate recommendations

ranked by impact
1
Overhead is the lever, not pricing.
4.12M/month, 56% of it salaries. The group only breaks even because AHD’s profit covers Designy’s loss. Every 10% trimmed ≈ +0.4M/month, straight to the bottom line — a bigger, more reliable gain than any pricing move.
2
Stop over-discounting AHD projects.
Deals you won averaged 23.6% discount; deals you lost, 24.8%. Discount doesn’t close projects — some of your biggest landed at 3–7%. Holding toward 15% ≈ +0.5M/month with no measured hit to win-rate.
3
Fix Designy’s unit economics — or cap it.
It loses ~0.9M/month: each module earns ~4k after material against ~10k of overhead. Raise price-per-module or cut the 47% material cost — discounting it only adds loss-making volume. Don’t scale it until the per-module math works.
4
Lean into Rotto and Bauhaus.
Rotto wardrobes throw off ~42.5k gross profit per module and Bauhaus ~30.8k — both well above a Maximalism kitchen’s ~20k. Weight sales and factory capacity toward your higher-margin lines, and convert more of those Bauhaus quotes that sit un-closed.
5
Don’t load factory overhead onto resale.
Appliances and marble run at 80% material — near pass-through. Charge them handling only; they each add a small positive margin and shouldn’t be made to look like losers.
6
Fix the lead follow-up before spending more on ads.
Your Meta leads cost ~1,100 EGP each, but 45% never answer and 28% sit unprocessed — only 1 of 289 reached a contract. The ad spend is fine; the conversion is the leak. Faster call-backs and disciplined follow-up turn the same spend from break-even to ~+600k/month. This is the cheapest growth lever you have.

Recommendation
Cap AHD project discounts at 15%, and make anything deeper an exception that must be earned.
1. Set a hard 15% ceiling on AHD quotes. Above it requires manager sign-off and a written reason. The data shows no win-rate gain beyond ~15% — lost deals were discounted more than won ones.
2. Re-train the pitch: sell design, not price. Reps should open on quality, lead time and finish — discount is a closing concession of last resort, not an opener.
3. Tier by buyer signal, not deal size. A buyer who has chosen AHD pays near list; reserve real depth only for a genuine price-shopper who is about to walk.
4. Make discipline visible. Review each rep’s average discount and win-rate monthly; reward margin held, not revenue booked at any cost.
Expected impact: moving the book from ~27% toward 15% adds ~0.5M/month with no measured loss in close rate.
Honest caveat: this matches quotes to orders at the customer level (the files don’t tie a single quote to its order), so it can’t prove discount never rescues a specific price-sensitive buyer. But across 172 high-ticket deals the pattern is consistent — the discount isn’t what closes them, so projects that would land anyway are leaving margin on the table.

9 · What the numbers say

the four things that decide profit
AHD: you’re over-discounting
At the deal level, discount doesn’t win AHD projects: the high-ticket deals you won averaged 23.6% discount, the ones you lost 24.8% — lost deals were discounted more, and some big projects closed at 3–7%. Holding firmer toward 15% adds ~0.5M/month with no hit to win-rate. Test it deal by deal.
Designy: structural loss
Each Designy module earns ~4k after material against ~10k of overhead. Its volume does follow discount (+0.54), but more volume can’t close a per-module gap that size. The fix is price-per-module up or the 47% material cost down — not discounting.
Overhead is the lever
4.12M/month, of which salaries are 52%. The group only reaches breakeven because AHD’s profit covers Designy’s loss. Pricing is a small dial next to this — every 100k/mo trimmed from overhead drops almost straight to profit.
Don’t over-charge resale
Appliances and marble run at 80% material — near pass-through. They never touch the factory, so charging them factory overhead makes them look like losers when they actually add a small positive margin. Charge them handling only.
